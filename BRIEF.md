# What US regional banks started worrying about after March 2023

**A one-page brief for risk, compliance and competitive-intelligence teams**

---

## The question

Every US public bank discloses its risk factors in the annual 10-K — twenty to
sixty pages, rewritten every year. Nobody tracks the rewrites, so a simple
question goes unanswered: **when something happens in the industry, how many
peers change what they disclose, how fast, and does it stick?**

We read 250 annual reports from 50 US regional banks holding $10–250 billion in
assets, covering 2021 to 2025, and identified every risk factor that appeared
with no counterpart in the prior year.

---

## The finding

**Between 8 and 13 of 50 banks newly disclosed a deposit-concentration or
FDIC-assessment risk in their FY2023 annual report** — the first filed after
Silicon Valley Bank's failure in March 2023.

The response was **fast, narrow and permanent**. It appears in the first filing
season after the event, not the second. It covers roughly a quarter of the panel
— two-thirds of these banks changed nothing about how they described deposit
risk. And the language added in FY2023 was still there in FY2025; nobody quietly
removed it once the news cycle passed.

The signal is specific to deposits. Over the same period, newly disclosed
cybersecurity risks *declined*, and artificial-intelligence risks did not appear
at scale until two years later.

| Share of banks newly disclosing | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deposits / liquidity** | 4% | **34%** | 2% | 0% |
| Cybersecurity | 14% | 8% | 2% | 2% |
| Artificial intelligence | 0% | 10% | 26% | 26% |
| Interest rates | 16% | 20% | 0% | 0% |

That divergence matters. Had every theme spiked in FY2023, the result would more
likely reflect a change in filing conventions than a change in what banks were
worried about.

---

## The observation worth acting on

**Five of the banks filed near-identical wording:**

> *"The proportion of our deposit account balances that exceed FDIC insurance
> limits may expose the Bank to enhanced liquidity risk in times of financial
> distress."*

Same sentence, different companies, same year.

This is disclosure counsel, not independent judgement — and it matters for
anyone benchmarking against peers. **A risk factor appearing across several
filings is not five institutions independently concluding they are exposed.** It
may be one law firm's template moving through the market.

For a compliance function asking *"are we disclosing what our industry
discloses?"*, industry practice is partly a drafting convention. Peer benchmarks
are evidence about market norms, not about underlying exposure.

---

## What this cannot tell you

**These are the banks that survived.** Companies were identified from their
December 2025 balance sheets, so a bank had to still exist in 2026 to appear.
Silicon Valley Bank, Signature Bank and First Republic are absent by
construction. This describes how *survivors* responded, not the sector.

**Disclosure is not exposure.** A bank that added a deposit risk factor is
telling you what it chose to write down. Banks with real exposure may have said
nothing; banks with little exposure may have added the paragraph because peers
did.

**The method has a measured error rate.** Deciding whether a reworded paragraph
is last year's risk in new words is imperfect: across all five years, roughly 44%
of items the system flags as new turn out to be existing risks rewritten.

The figures above are not taken from that automated output. Every risk behind
this finding was checked by hand against the company's full prior-year
disclosure, and four of nineteen candidates were rejected on inspection.

The range of 8 to 13 gives both a strict count — filings explicitly naming
uninsured deposits or the 2023 failures — and a broader one including
generically-worded liquidity additions. Showing the sensitivity is more useful
than picking one number.

---

*Source: SEC EDGAR, public 10-K filings, FY2021–FY2025. The full method, its
accuracy measurements and the reasoning behind every judgement call are
published alongside the data.*
